# 1. Introduction

## 1.1 Purpose of RAH Lab

RAH Lab is the internal engineering and operational environment responsible for preparing, validating, deploying, and maintaining software systems and their supporting infrastructure within Al-Rassoul Al-Aazam Hospital (RAH).

The laboratory provides a controlled environment through which software received from internal or external developers can be prepared for hospital deployment, validated under representative conditions, released for offline operation, and maintained throughout its operational lifecycle.

RAH Lab also supports the engineering and maintenance of the **RAH Offline Infrastructure Platform (RAH-OIP)**, which provides the standardized Debian-based infrastructure required to install, update, recover, and maintain systems operating within the hospital's air-gapped environment.

The objective of RAH Lab is to ensure that application and infrastructure deployment does not depend on individual knowledge, undocumented actions, or improvised procedures. Installation, validation, update, recovery, and maintenance activities must instead follow documented and repeatable engineering processes appropriate to the hospital's operational and information-security constraints.

---

## 1.2 Purpose of this Manual

This manual defines the **operational procedures and responsibilities of the RAH Lab engineer** when receiving, validating, installing, updating, operating, and maintaining software and infrastructure within the hospital environment.

It describes how an operator interacts with the RAH Lab environments and approved release artifacts, including application installation and update, production validation, database access and maintenance, backup and recovery, deployment documentation, and other recurring operational activities.

This manual is not intended to redefine the engineering requirements, architecture, or construction procedures of the systems it operates. Those responsibilities are defined by the corresponding RAH software engineering, infrastructure, application release and deployment, application engineering, model management, and offline infrastructure platform standards.

The objective of this manual is to make routine RAH Lab operations reproducible by future engineers without requiring undocumented knowledge from the person who originally developed or deployed a system. An approved release should therefore be operable using its documented procedures and the standardized RAH Lab environment rather than relying on project-specific knowledge or repeated consultation with the original developer.

---

## 1.3 Scope

This manual applies to software systems and supporting infrastructure deployed or maintained through RAH Lab, including internally developed applications and systems received from external contributors.

It covers the **operational lifecycle after software or infrastructure enters the RAH Lab workflow**, including:

- receiving and identifying approved release artifacts;
- validating releases within the appropriate laboratory environment;
- installing and updating applications in the offline environment;
- performing production deployment and post-deployment verification;
- accessing and maintaining deployed databases using approved operational tools;
- locating and using deployment documentation and operational credentials;
- performing backup, recovery, troubleshooting, and routine maintenance;
- operating infrastructure releases required to maintain the air-gapped Debian platform.

This manual may provide enough architectural context for an operator to understand the environment and perform these activities safely. It does not define application architecture, business logic, software implementation requirements, release construction procedures, containerization methodology, or infrastructure engineering requirements. Those subjects belong to their respective engineering standards and playbooks.

Project-specific behavior and exceptional operational requirements should be documented within the corresponding application's technical or deployment documentation.

# 2. RAH Operational Environments

## 2.1 Online and Offline Operating Model

RAH Lab operates across two intentionally separated environments: an **Online Engineering Environment** and an **Offline Production Environment**.

The **Online Engineering Environment** provides Internet-connected resources required for software and infrastructure engineering. It is used to prepare, build, test, and validate software and infrastructure before they are transferred into production.

The **Offline Production Environment** hosts hospital operational systems and processes real clinical and administrative data. It remains isolated from the public Internet and receives software or infrastructure only through the hospital's approved offline transfer process.

For operational purposes, RAH Lab engineers shall treat the transfer between these environments as a controlled boundary:

**Online Engineering → Validation → Approved Release Artifact → Offline Transfer → Offline Deployment**

The detailed architecture, network topology, machine specifications, and infrastructure design of these environments are defined by the corresponding RAH infrastructure documentation.

---

## 2.2 Engineering and Validation Environments

Before production deployment, RAH Lab uses dedicated engineering and validation environments for different purposes.

### Application Engineering Environment

The application engineering environment is used to prepare and validate hospital applications before offline deployment.

Typical activities include:

- receiving or cloning application source code;
- application development and modification;
- building application container images;
- local functional testing;
- preparing Application Releases;
- performing customer or stakeholder validation where required.

The current primary application engineering workstation is the **Lenovo Legion**.

---

### Online Debian Infrastructure Builder

The Online Debian Infrastructure Builder is used for engineering and preparing the **RAH Offline Infrastructure Platform (RAH-OIP)** and its Infrastructure Releases.

Typical activities include:

- collecting approved Debian packages;
- building and maintaining the Local Package Repository;
- preparing required infrastructure container images;
- preparing installation and verification tooling;
- assembling Infrastructure Releases;
- generating infrastructure release documentation.

This environment is Internet-connected and is used only for infrastructure engineering and release preparation.

---

### Offline Debian Validation Environment

The Offline Debian Validation Environment is an air-gapped laboratory environment used to verify that releases operate correctly without Internet connectivity before they are introduced into production.

Typical activities include:

- validating offline installation procedures;
- testing package installation from approved offline repositories;
- validating container deployment;
- testing Application Releases where appropriate;
- validating Infrastructure Releases;
- executing verification procedures;
- confirming that required runtime dependencies are available offline;
- confirming that releases can be installed and operated without Internet access.

The validation environment shall remain representative of the offline production conditions relevant to the release being tested.

---

## 2.3 Offline Production Environment

The Offline Production Environment is the authoritative operational environment for hospital software systems.

It may contain:

- hospital application servers;
- production Docker services;
- production databases;
- shared infrastructure services;
- hospital workstations;
- shared AI services and GPU infrastructure.

Systems within the hospital network may communicate with one another according to the approved network architecture while remaining isolated from the public Internet.

Only approved Application Releases, Infrastructure Releases, and other authorized artifacts may cross into this environment through the hospital's approved offline transfer procedure.

Production data shall remain within the approved offline environment unless an explicitly authorized procedure states otherwise.

---

## 2.4 Shared Operational Services

Hospital applications may consume shared infrastructure services where this is appropriate to their architecture.

Current or planned shared services may include:

- Microsoft SQL Server;
- Speech-to-Text (STT) services;
- AI inference services;
- shared GPU resources;
- shared container infrastructure;
- future authentication or infrastructure services.

The presence of shared services does not remove the requirement for each application to have a documented and reproducible deployment procedure.

The **Offline GPU Platform** provides centralized computational resources for workloads such as Speech-to-Text, AI inference, future Large Language Models, and medical image processing where these services are approved for hospital use.

---

## 2.5 Operational Role Mapping

Hardware may change over time. Operational procedures should therefore refer primarily to **roles** rather than depending on a particular physical machine.

|Operational Role|Current Platform|
|---|---|
|General Engineering Workstation|HP ProBook|
|Application Engineering Workstation|Lenovo Legion|
|Infrastructure Builder|Online Debian Infrastructure Builder|
|Offline Validation Environment|Offline Debian Validation Environment|
|Offline Production Platform|Offline Hospital Infrastructure|
|Shared AI Platform|Offline GPU Platform|

The current machine specifications, network addresses, operating-system versions, software versions, and other environment-specific information shall be maintained in the **RAH-OIP Lab Environment Reference** or the corresponding infrastructure reference documentation rather than duplicated in this manual.

---

## 2.6 Operational Flow

From the operator's perspective, software and infrastructure follow two related but distinct release paths.

### Application Release Path

**Application Engineering → Application Release → Offline Validation → Approved Offline Transfer → Production Installation or Update → Production Verification → Operational Maintenance**

### Infrastructure Release Path

**Infrastructure Engineering → Infrastructure Release → Offline Validation → Publication → Approved Offline Transfer → Infrastructure Installation or Update → Verification**

Application Releases deliver or update hospital applications.

Infrastructure Releases deliver or update the underlying offline Debian platform and shared infrastructure required to operate those applications.

The detailed requirements for constructing these release artifacts are defined by their respective engineering standards. This manual governs the operational handling, validation, deployment, and maintenance of approved releases.

# 3. Application Operational Workflow

This chapter defines the standard operational workflow used by RAH Lab when a software application enters laboratory control and progresses toward offline production deployment and long-term maintenance.

Application engineering requirements, containerization methodology, and the construction of Application Releases are defined by the corresponding RAH engineering standards and playbooks. The Operator Manual begins from the perspective of the engineer responsible for receiving the application or its release artifacts, validating their readiness, deploying approved releases, and maintaining the resulting production installation.

Unless an approved exception exists, applications maintained by RAH Lab shall pass through a documented validation and deployment process before being introduced into the Offline Production Environment.

---

## 3.1 Project Acceptance and Operational Handover

A software project may originate from an internal development team, an external contributor, or an existing hospital system entering RAH Lab maintenance.

Before RAH Lab assumes operational responsibility for an application, sufficient information and artifacts shall be available to understand how the system is built, released, deployed, operated, and recovered.

The following should be obtained or identified whenever applicable:

- source code repository and current maintained branch;
- project and technical documentation;
- current application version;
- technology stack and required services;
- database platform and database requirements;
- application configuration requirements;
- external and third-party dependencies;
- existing deployment instructions;
- backup and recovery requirements;
- current production deployment information, when the application already exists;
- responsible developer or technical contact where applicable.

Missing information that prevents reproducible deployment or maintenance shall be identified before the application is considered operationally ready.

Acceptance into the RAH Lab portfolio does not by itself authorize production deployment. The application must still satisfy the applicable engineering, release, validation, and operational requirements.

---

## 3.2 Engineering Preparation

Applications requiring development, correction, containerization, or release preparation are handled within the Online Engineering Environment according to the applicable RAH engineering standards.

Engineering activities may include:

- software modification and defect correction;
- deployment-readiness improvements;
- containerization;
- external configuration preparation;
- database installation and migration engineering;
- offline dependency preparation;
- production configuration support;
- Application Release construction;
- release verification.

These activities are engineering responsibilities rather than production operations.

The output expected by the operator is an identifiable **Application Release** that has been prepared for the intended deployment environment and is ready for controlled validation.

An additional online copy of the production database is not inherently required for this release process. Database preparation shall follow the application's approved installation and migration strategy.

---

## 3.3 Application Release Handover

An Application Release is the versioned delivery artifact used to install or update an application. It is separate from the application's persistent live installation.

The operator shall be able to identify at minimum:

- application identity;
- release version;
- intended deployment or update purpose;
- required application container images;
- deployment configuration;
- database installation or migration resources;
- installation and update procedures;
- release documentation;
- integrity information where required;
- infrastructure prerequisites or dependencies.

The Application Release shall not be treated as the permanent operational location of the installed application.

After deployment, the application's persistent live state is maintained under the standardized RAH application hierarchy:

`/opt/rah/apps/<app-slug>/`

Persistent configuration, operational state, and other information required by future updates shall not depend on retaining the original versioned Release Package.

The detailed structure and engineering requirements of an Application Release are defined by the **RAH Application Release & Deployment Standard**.

---

## 3.4 Infrastructure Prerequisites

An application may require infrastructure capabilities that are not yet available in the target offline environment.

Examples may include:

- required Debian packages;
- Docker or Docker Compose capabilities;
- shared infrastructure containers;
- system-level services;
- approved runtime dependencies.

When infrastructure changes are required, they shall be delivered through an independently versioned **Infrastructure Release** prepared and validated according to the RAH Offline Infrastructure Platform standards.

Application Releases and Infrastructure Releases are separate engineering products. An application update does not automatically require an Infrastructure Release.

Before application deployment, the operator shall confirm that the target environment satisfies the infrastructure prerequisites declared by the Application Release.

---

## 3.5 Offline Validation

Before production deployment, the release shall be validated under conditions representative of the Offline Production Environment.

Validation should confirm, where applicable:

- release integrity;
- successful offline installation or update;
- availability of all required runtime dependencies without Internet access;
- successful container startup;
- database creation or migration;
- communication between application services;
- persistence of application data and configuration;
- accessibility through the intended client or workstation path;
- integration with required shared services;
- generation or availability of operational deployment documentation;
- database accessibility through the approved RAH database administration tooling;
- application-specific functionality required for deployment acceptance.

Validation shall test the application through its intended operational access path rather than relying only on development shortcuts such as `localhost`.

Applications using browser capabilities that require a secure context, including microphone access where applicable, shall be validated using the same HTTPS and workstation trust model expected in production.

A release that cannot operate correctly under representative offline conditions shall not proceed to production deployment.

---

## 3.6 Offline Transfer and Production Installation

After successful validation and approval, the required release artifacts are transferred into the Offline Production Environment using the hospital's approved offline transfer procedure and approved physical media.

Before installation, the operator shall:

1. identify the application and release version;
2. verify that the release is intended for the target environment;
3. verify release integrity where applicable;
4. confirm required infrastructure prerequisites;
5. identify whether the operation is a fresh installation or an update;
6. confirm that required backups or rollback prerequisites have been prepared.

Normal application installation shall use the release-provided installation procedure rather than manually reconstructing the deployment.

A successful fresh installation is expected to establish the complete operational deployment, including where applicable:

- the live application under `/opt/rah/apps/<app-slug>/`;
- required application services;
- production database initialization;
- persistent application configuration;
- generated machine and service credentials;
- required operator-facing application access;
- database registration in the approved DBeaver environment;
- deployment documentation under the RAH operational documentation hierarchy;
- required HTTPS certificate and client trust configuration;
- required managed browser permissions for approved application functionality.

The operator should not normally need to manually discover database ports, credentials, service addresses, or other deployment parameters after installation.

---

## 3.7 Production Validation

Production installation is not considered complete merely because application containers are running.

Following deployment, the operator shall verify the application from the perspective of its actual production use.

Validation should include, where applicable:

- application services are running correctly;
- production database connectivity is functional;
- expected persistent data is available;
- the application is reachable from intended hospital workstations;
- required shared infrastructure or AI services are accessible;
- HTTPS access is trusted by supported client workstations;
- browser-dependent functionality operates correctly;
- microphone functionality operates without unnecessary user configuration where required;
- the application's database connection is available through DBeaver;
- generated deployment documentation accurately describes the installed system;
- the deployed version and installation result have been recorded;
- representative application functionality operates correctly.

Only after these checks succeed shall the deployment be considered operationally complete.

---

## 3.8 Application Updates

Application updates shall be delivered through a new versioned Application Release and applied to the existing live installation.

The update process shall preserve persistent production state while replacing or modifying only the components required by the new release.

Before an update, the operator shall:

- identify the currently installed version;
- identify the incoming release version;
- review release and migration information;
- verify required infrastructure prerequisites;
- create the required production backup;
- confirm that rollback information is available.

The release-provided update procedure shall then perform the required application update, database migration, service recreation, configuration preservation, and verification activities.

An update shall also bring operational integration into the current RAH deployment standard where applicable. This includes ensuring that:

- deployment documentation is updated;
- release history records the new version and update result;
- the application's DBeaver connection exists and reflects the current deployment;
- required workstation trust or application-access configuration remains functional.

The operator shall not manually transfer persistent configuration between old and new Release Package directories. Persistent configuration belongs to the stable live installation and shall survive release updates through the standardized deployment mechanism.

---

## 3.9 Rollback and Recovery

Every production update shall have a defined recovery strategy appropriate to the application and the changes being introduced.

Before applying an update, the operator shall ensure that the required recovery materials exist. These may include:

- a verified production database backup;
- the previous Application Release;
- previous application container images;
- current persistent deployment configuration;
- database migration recovery information;
- an applicable Infrastructure Release or infrastructure recovery procedure where infrastructure changes are involved.

If an update fails and cannot be safely corrected, recovery may require:

1. stopping the failed application services;
2. restoring the previous application version;
3. restoring the previous deployment configuration where required;
4. restoring the production database where required;
5. restoring affected infrastructure where applicable;
6. restarting the previous operational version;
7. validating production functionality;
8. recording the failed deployment and corrective action in the application's operational documentation.

The exact rollback procedure may differ between applications, particularly when database migrations or infrastructure changes are involved. The operator shall therefore follow the recovery procedure provided for the specific release rather than assuming that container rollback alone is sufficient.

---

## 3.10 Operational Lifecycle

The standard application lifecycle from the RAH Lab operator's perspective is:

**Project Acceptance → Engineering Preparation → Application Release → Infrastructure Prerequisite Check → Offline Validation → Approved Offline Transfer → Production Installation or Update → Production Validation → Operational Maintenance**

Infrastructure Releases follow their own independently versioned engineering and validation lifecycle and are introduced into the application workflow only when the target environment requires infrastructure changes.

The objective of this workflow is not merely to place software into production, but to leave behind an installation that is reproducible, maintainable, documented, inspectable, and ready for future versioned updates.

# 4. Database Operations

Production databases are among the most critical components of software systems maintained by RAH Lab. Unlike replaceable application containers or Release Packages, production databases contain persistent hospital information that must survive application installation, updates, migrations, container recreation, and infrastructure maintenance.

Database operations shall therefore distinguish clearly between:

- initial database installation;
- routine application operation;
- database schema migration;
- backup and recovery;
- database inspection and administration.

The primary operational objective is to allow applications to evolve while preserving the integrity, availability, and continuity of production data.

---

## 4.1 Database Deployment Models

Microsoft SQL Server is the preferred relational database platform for internally developed RAH applications unless the application architecture or an approved engineering decision requires another platform.

Applications may use different database deployment models depending on their architecture.

Typical models include:

- a shared database server hosting independent application databases;
- a dedicated database service deployed for an individual application;
- another approved database platform required by an existing application.

Example of a shared database service:

```text
Shared SQL Server
│
├── Patient_Complaints_DB
├── Operating_Room_DB
├── Blood_Bank_DB
├── Indicators_DB
└── Future_Project_DB
```

Applications should maintain clear logical separation between their databases even when they share common database infrastructure.

Production databases and their operational data remain within the approved Offline Production Environment.

---

## 4.2 Engineering and Production Data

Software engineering may use development, test, synthetic, or other approved non-production databases when required for application development and validation.

The Application Release process does not require RAH Lab to maintain a separate online replica of every production database. Applications shall instead provide the installation and migration resources required to establish and evolve their database within the target offline environment.

The Offline Production Environment contains the authoritative operational databases used by hospital applications.

Production databases contain real hospital information and shall remain subject to the hospital's access, backup, transfer, and information-security requirements.

Production data shall not be copied into the Online Engineering Environment unless an explicitly approved procedure authorizes that transfer.

---

## 4.3 Initial Database Installation

A fresh application deployment may require the creation and initialization of a new production database.

The corresponding Application Release shall provide or automate the resources required to establish the database in a reproducible manner.

Depending on the application, this may include:

- database schema;
- lookup and seed data;
- required database users, roles, and permissions;
- initial application administrator information;
- database version information;
- required installation or initialization scripts.

The installation procedure shall establish the database without requiring the operator to manually reconstruct its schema or rediscover application connection parameters.

A typical installation sequence is:

1. establish the required database service or identify the approved shared database service;
2. create the application database where required;
3. apply the initial schema;
4. install required lookup or seed data;
5. configure required database users, roles, and permissions;
6. establish the initial database version;
7. connect the deployed application;
8. register the database connection in the approved DBeaver environment;
9. record the database connection and deployment information in the application's operational documentation;
10. verify successful application and database operation.

Machine or service credentials required by the deployment may be generated automatically by the installation procedure. Operator-facing application credentials may follow the application's approved deployment convention.

After installation, operational data is created and maintained within the Offline Production Environment.

---

## 4.4 Database Access and DBeaver

DBeaver is the standard operator-facing database administration and inspection tool used by RAH Lab for supported application databases.

A deployment that contains an operator-accessible database should provide a usable DBeaver connection as part of its operational setup.

The operator should not normally need to rediscover:

- database platform;
- server address;
- port;
- database name;
- database user;
- database password;
- other routine connection parameters.

These values shall be made available through the standardized deployment process and the application's operational documentation.

For a fresh installation, the deployment procedure shall ensure that the required DBeaver connection is available.

For an application update, the deployment procedure shall verify or repair the DBeaver registration where applicable. This allows applications deployed before adoption of the current standard to be brought into compliance during a later controlled update.

The exact mechanism used by release tooling to create or maintain DBeaver connections is an implementation concern defined by the corresponding engineering playbook.

---

## 4.5 Database Updates and Migrations

After initial production deployment, an existing production database shall not be recreated merely to deploy a new application version.

Schema and data-structure changes shall instead be applied through controlled, versioned migration procedures designed to preserve existing production information.

Typical migrations may include:

- creating tables;
- adding or modifying columns;
- creating indexes;
- adding or modifying constraints;
- updating lookup data;
- updating stored procedures or views;
- performing approved data transformations;
- correcting schema structures.

Database migrations form part of the corresponding Application Release and shall be compatible with the application version being deployed.

Before a migration is executed, the operator shall confirm that:

- the intended source and target application versions are known;
- the required production backup has been created;
- the migration belongs to the incoming Application Release;
- an appropriate recovery strategy exists.

The release-provided update procedure should perform the required migration rather than requiring the operator to manually reproduce database changes.

After migration, both application operation and database accessibility shall be verified.

---

## 4.6 Database Version and Deployment Traceability

Database evolution shall remain traceable to the Application Release that introduced it.

A deployment record should identify, where applicable:

- application name;
- deployed application version;
- database platform;
- database or schema version;
- migration applied;
- deployment or migration date;
- previous version;
- migration result;
- associated backup or recovery information.

These records form part of the application's operational deployment documentation maintained under the RAH documentation hierarchy.

The purpose of version traceability is to determine what application and database state is currently deployed and what changes were introduced by each version.

Individual backend, frontend, database, or container artifacts do not need to use identical version identifiers where the application architecture does not require it. The authoritative relationship between deployable components shall instead be defined by the corresponding versioned Application Release.

---

## 4.7 Supported Database Platforms

Microsoft SQL Server is the preferred relational database platform for internally developed RAH applications.

Existing or externally developed applications may use other database platforms, including PostgreSQL, MariaDB, MySQL, or another approved technology.

The database platform used by an application shall be documented as part of its deployment information.

When an existing application uses another database platform, the choice to retain that platform or migrate to Microsoft SQL Server is an engineering decision that should consider:

- long-term maintainability;
- compatibility with the offline deployment environment;
- backup and recovery support;
- migration complexity and risk;
- operational support requirements;
- engineering effort.

The Operator Manual does not require database migration solely for the purpose of standardization. Once a database platform has been approved for an application, the operator shall follow the corresponding documented deployment, backup, recovery, and maintenance procedures.

---

## 4.8 Database Backup and Recovery

Production databases shall be protected through documented backup and recovery procedures.

A verified backup should be created before operations that may modify or endanger persistent production data, including:

- application updates involving database changes;
- database migrations;
- infrastructure maintenance affecting database services;
- major database maintenance;
- other operations identified by the application's recovery procedure.

A new empty database created during a fresh first-time installation does not require a pre-installation backup. Once operational data exists, however, subsequent changes shall follow the applicable backup policy.

Whenever practical, backup integrity and recoverability should be verified before potentially destructive production changes are applied.

The location, date, purpose, and relevant application version of deployment-related backups should be identifiable through the application's operational documentation.

No production migration or other significant structural database change shall proceed without an appropriate recovery strategy.

---

## 4.9 Database Operational Principles

Database operations within RAH Lab follow these principles:

- production data is persistent operational state and shall be preserved across application releases;
- application updates shall not recreate production databases as a normal deployment mechanism;
- database changes shall be controlled, versioned, and associated with the corresponding Application Release;
- database migrations shall be preceded by an appropriate backup and recovery plan;
- production database information shall remain traceable through deployment documentation;
- approved databases shall be accessible through standardized operator tooling such as DBeaver where applicable;
- operators should not need to rediscover routine database connection information after deployment;
- production data shall remain within the approved offline environment unless an authorized procedure explicitly permits otherwise.

These principles allow RAH applications and their database schemas to evolve while preserving operational continuity and making deployed database systems understandable and maintainable by future RAH Lab engineers.

# 5. Docker Operations

Docker is the standard container platform used by RAH Lab to operate hospital applications within the Offline Production Environment.

Applications are normally delivered as prebuilt container images through approved Application Releases. The operator is responsible for installing, starting, stopping, updating, verifying, and troubleshooting these deployments using the procedures provided by the release.

Application containers shall not normally be built or modified within the Offline Production Environment.

The engineering requirements for containerizing applications, constructing Docker images, preparing Docker Compose definitions, and building Application Releases are defined by the corresponding RAH engineering standards and playbooks.

---

## 5.1 Operational Container Model

A deployed application may consist of one or more containerized services.

Typical services include:

- backend application;
- frontend application;
- reverse proxy;
- background worker;
- database service where applicable;
- other application-specific services.

Example:

```text
Application
│
├── Reverse Proxy
├── Frontend
├── Backend
└── Database
```

Some applications may instead consume shared database, AI, Speech-to-Text, or other infrastructure services operating independently from the application's own containers.

From an operational perspective, the complete set of services defined for an application shall be treated as a single deployed application system.

---

## 5.2 Docker Compose Operations

Docker Compose is the standard operational interface for containerized RAH applications.

Each deployed application has a stable deployment identity and operational location independent of the versioned Release Package from which it was installed.

The standard application root is:

```text
/opt/rah/apps/<app-slug>/
```

The deployed application maintains the Docker Compose configuration and persistent deployment configuration required for future operation and updates within its stable application area.

The operator should use the application's approved deployment procedures rather than running Compose against arbitrary copies of files contained in old or incoming Release Packages.

Typical operational activities include:

- checking application service status;
- starting application services;
- stopping application services;
- restarting services;
- reviewing container logs;
- verifying container health;
- applying release-provided updates;
- confirming successful service recreation after an update.

The exact Compose commands required for installation and update are implemented by the application's release tooling and engineering procedures.

---

## 5.3 Application Identity and Isolation

Multiple applications may operate on the same Docker host.

Each application shall maintain a stable and independent deployment identity so that its containers, networks, configuration, and other Docker resources are not unintentionally coupled to the directory name or version of an incoming Release Package.

Operationally, this means that:

- updating one application shall not unintentionally create a second deployment of the same application;
- a new Release Package shall update the existing live installation rather than create a version-dependent Docker project;
- application services shall remain distinguishable from services belonging to other applications;
- persistent application state shall remain associated with the live installation across updates.

The detailed Compose project naming and path conventions used to enforce this behavior are defined by the Application Release & Deployment Standard and Application Engineering Playbook.

---

## 5.4 Persistent Configuration

Environment-specific configuration shall remain outside immutable application images.

The live deployment may maintain configuration such as:

- database connection information;
- service endpoints;
- application environment settings;
- network ports;
- authentication settings;
- machine and service credentials;
- API or AI service endpoints;
- logging configuration.

Persistent deployment configuration shall remain associated with the stable live application under `/opt/rah/apps/<app-slug>/` and shall survive versioned Application Release updates.

The operator shall not manually copy `.env` or equivalent persistent configuration from an old Release Package into a new Release Package as part of the normal update process.

Where machine or service credentials are generated during installation, the deployment process shall preserve the authoritative configuration required by the application and make the corresponding operational information discoverable through the standardized RAH deployment documentation.

---

## 5.5 Persistent Data

Application containers shall be replaceable without destroying operational data.

Persistent information may be stored using:

- Microsoft SQL Server;
- PostgreSQL or another approved database platform;
- Docker volumes;
- approved persistent host directories;
- shared infrastructure services.

Container recreation, application restart, or application image replacement shall not be treated as permission to delete persistent production data.

Before an update involving database or other persistent-state changes, the operator shall follow the applicable backup and recovery procedure.

---

## 5.6 Application Installation and Updates

Docker images used in production are delivered through versioned Application Releases and shall not normally be rebuilt inside the Offline Production Environment.

For a fresh installation, the release-provided installation procedure is responsible for establishing the live deployment and its required Docker resources.

For an update, the release-provided update procedure operates against the existing stable application deployment.

The update mechanism shall preserve persistent configuration and data while replacing or recreating the application services required by the incoming release.

Application services may be deliberately recreated during an update to prevent stale or previously failed container state from surviving into the new deployment. Persistent database or infrastructure services shall follow their corresponding lifecycle procedures and shall not be treated as disposable application containers.

The operator should use the provided installation and update procedures rather than manually reconstructing the required Docker operations.

---

## 5.7 Docker Verification and Troubleshooting

After installation, update, restart, or recovery, the operator shall verify the deployed application rather than relying solely on successful script completion.

Docker-level verification may include:

- confirming that expected containers exist;
- confirming that required containers are running;
- reviewing container health status where health checks are available;
- reviewing logs for startup or runtime errors;
- confirming required Docker networks and persistent storage remain available;
- confirming that expected ports are available and correctly published;
- confirming that application services can communicate with required dependencies.

Docker health alone does not establish that an application is operationally complete.

The operator shall also perform the corresponding application-level production validation, including database connectivity, workstation accessibility, shared-service integration, HTTPS access, browser functionality, and other application-specific checks where applicable.

When troubleshooting a failed deployment, the operator should preserve the relevant logs and deployment information before making uncontrolled manual changes to the live environment.

---

## 5.8 Docker Operational Principles

Docker operations within RAH Lab follow these principles:

- production uses prebuilt and validated application images;
- application images are not normally rebuilt in the Offline Production Environment;
- each application has a stable deployment identity and live operational location;
- versioned Release Packages do not become the application's permanent runtime location;
- persistent configuration survives application updates;
- persistent production data remains outside replaceable application containers;
- application updates use the approved release-provided procedures;
- application services may be recreated when required without treating persistent data as disposable;
- multiple applications sharing a Docker host remain operationally isolated;
- Docker-level verification is followed by application-level production validation.

These principles allow containerized hospital applications to be updated and maintained predictably while preserving the persistent configuration and production data required for long-term operation.

# 6. Release Operations

RAH Lab uses versioned release artifacts to introduce controlled software and infrastructure changes into the Offline Production Environment.

From an operational perspective, a release is an approved and identifiable package that can be transferred, validated, installed or applied, recorded, and retained for future recovery or traceability.

RAH Lab operates with two distinct release types:

- **Application Releases**, which install or update individual software applications.
- **Infrastructure Releases**, which install or update components of the RAH Offline Infrastructure Platform (RAH-OIP).

The engineering requirements used to construct, package, and validate these artifacts are defined by their respective release and engineering standards. This chapter defines how the RAH Lab operator handles approved releases.

---

## 6.1 Release Types

### Application Release

An Application Release is the versioned delivery artifact used to install or update an individual application.

Depending on the application, it may contain:

- application container images;
- deployment configuration;
- installation and update tooling;
- database installation or migration resources;
- release notes;
- integrity information;
- deployment documentation;
- other application-specific resources required for offline deployment.

Each application maintains its own independent release history.

An Application Release is a **delivery artifact**, not the permanent live location of the installed application.

After installation, persistent application deployment state is maintained under:

```text
/opt/rah/apps/<app-slug>/
```

The incoming versioned Release Package may therefore be archived or removed from its temporary transfer location after successful deployment according to the applicable retention procedure, without affecting the live installation.

---

### Infrastructure Release

An Infrastructure Release is a versioned artifact produced through the **RAH Offline Infrastructure Platform (RAH-OIP)**.

It is used to install or update the Debian-based infrastructure required to operate applications within the hospital's air-gapped environment.

Infrastructure Releases may contain:

- offline Debian package repositories;
- Docker runtime components;
- approved infrastructure container images;
- infrastructure utilities;
- installation and update tooling;
- verification tooling;
- release documentation;
- manifests and integrity information.

Infrastructure Releases are versioned independently from Application Releases.

An Application Release shall not be treated as an Infrastructure Release, and an application update does not require an Infrastructure Release unless the target environment lacks an infrastructure capability required by that application.

---

## 6.2 Release Identification

Before any installation or update, the operator shall identify the release being handled.

At minimum, the operator should be able to determine:

- release type;
- application or infrastructure identity;
- release version;
- intended operation, such as fresh installation or update;
- target environment;
- required prerequisites;
- release integrity information where applicable;
- release notes or known operational considerations;
- recovery or rollback information.

Application and Infrastructure Releases maintain independent version histories.

For example:

```text
Application: STT Schedule
Application Release: 1.4.0

Target Platform:
RAH-OIP 1.x
```

A release version identifies the release as a complete compatible deployment unit. Individual internal components may use their own artifact versions where required by the application architecture.

The operator shall rely on the Application Release to define the compatible set of application components rather than manually assembling backend, frontend, database, or configuration versions from unrelated releases.

---

## 6.3 Release Validation Before Deployment

Before a release enters production, the operator shall confirm that it has completed the required validation process.

For an Application Release, validation should establish where applicable that:

- the release can be installed or updated offline;
- required application images are available;
- required database installation or migration procedures operate correctly;
- persistent configuration survives the intended update path;
- required runtime dependencies are available without Internet access;
- application services start correctly;
- the application is reachable through its intended operational access path;
- required shared services are accessible;
- database administration access is available through the approved operational tooling;
- HTTPS, certificate trust, and browser-dependent functionality operate correctly where required;
- operational deployment documentation is produced or updated correctly.

For an Infrastructure Release, validation shall follow the RAH-OIP validation procedure before publication and production use.

A release that has not completed its required validation shall not be introduced into production as an approved release.

---

## 6.4 Production Installation and Update

Approved release artifacts are transferred into the Offline Production Environment using the hospital's approved offline transfer procedure.

The operator shall use the installation or update procedure provided by the release rather than manually reconstructing the deployment.

### Fresh Application Installation

A successful installation should establish the complete operational application environment where applicable, including:

- the live application deployment under `/opt/rah/apps/<app-slug>/`;
- required application services;
- persistent application configuration;
- database initialization;
- generated machine or service credentials;
- approved operator-facing application access;
- DBeaver database registration;
- deployment documentation;
- release-history information;
- required HTTPS certificate and workstation trust;
- required browser permissions for approved application functionality.

Normal installation should not require the operator to manually edit configuration files or rediscover routine deployment parameters between installation steps.

### Application Update

An update operates against the existing stable live deployment.

Before applying an update, the operator shall verify the required backup and recovery prerequisites.

The approved update procedure should, where applicable:

- preserve persistent application configuration;
- preserve production data;
- load the new application artifacts;
- apply required database migrations;
- recreate application services where required;
- preserve or repair operational integrations;
- ensure the application's DBeaver registration remains available;
- update deployment documentation;
- append the new release to the application's release history;
- perform post-update verification.

The operator shall not manually move persistent `.env` or equivalent configuration between versioned Release Package directories as part of the normal update process.

---

## 6.5 Operational Deployment Documentation

Every deployed application shall maintain operational documentation under the standardized RAH documentation hierarchy:

```text
/opt/rah/documentation/
```

This directory serves as the RAH operational **Obsidian Vault**.

Application-specific documentation is organized within the Vault so that an operator can determine the current deployment state without reconstructing information from Docker configuration, source code, old Release Packages, or previous engineering conversations.

Operational documentation should make relevant information discoverable, including where applicable:

- application name;
- current deployed version;
- installation date;
- most recent update date;
- live deployment location;
- application access URL;
- operator-facing credentials;
- database platform;
- database host and port;
- database name and user;
- required operational database credentials;
- DBeaver connection identity;
- relevant backup locations;
- release history;
- deployment result and verification information.

Because this documentation may contain operational credentials and other sensitive deployment information, the RAH documentation Vault shall be treated as controlled operational information and protected accordingly.

---

## 6.6 Release History and Deployment Records

Each application shall maintain a traceable history of its production installations and updates.

The standardized operational documentation should record, where applicable:

- application identity;
- Application Release version;
- deployment date and time;
- previous version;
- deployment type, such as fresh installation or update;
- database migration information;
- relevant Infrastructure Release or platform information;
- backup or recovery information;
- deployment result;
- verification result;
- responsible engineer where required;
- relevant notes or known issues.

The deployment process should generate or update these records automatically wherever practical.

The application-specific release history within the RAH documentation Vault is the primary operational record for understanding how the currently deployed application reached its present state.

A separate centralized summary or portfolio view may be maintained where useful, but it shall not replace the application-specific deployment history.

---

## 6.7 Release Retention and Immutability

Published Application Releases and Infrastructure Releases are immutable engineering artifacts.

A published release shall not be silently modified after publication. A correction or enhancement requires a new release version.

Approved releases required for rollback, disaster recovery, traceability, or reproducibility shall be retained according to the applicable RAH archive procedure.

The temporary copy of an Application Release used during installation is distinct from both:

- the persistent live installation under `/opt/rah/apps/<app-slug>/`; and
- the archived immutable Release Package retained for historical or recovery purposes.

This distinction prevents the operational application from depending on the continued presence or directory name of the package from which it was originally installed.

---

## 6.8 Deployment Completion

A release deployment is not complete merely because its installation script finishes or its containers start.

An Application Release is considered operationally deployed only after the required production checks succeed, including where applicable:

- application services are healthy;
- production data is available and preserved;
- database connectivity is functional;
- the application is reachable from intended workstations;
- required shared services are functional;
- DBeaver access is available;
- HTTPS and workstation trust are functional;
- required browser capabilities, including microphone access where applicable, operate with the approved workstation configuration;
- deployment documentation reflects the actual installed system;
- release history records the deployment;
- rollback or recovery information remains available.

Failed validation shall be recorded and handled according to the application's recovery procedure.

---

## 6.9 Release Operational Principles

Release operations within RAH Lab follow these principles:

- every production change is associated with an identifiable release;
- Application Releases and Infrastructure Releases remain independent release types;
- published release artifacts are immutable;
- releases are validated before production deployment;
- the Release Package is separate from the persistent live installation;
- application installation and update use standardized release-provided procedures;
- persistent configuration and production data survive versioned application updates;
- deployment information is automatically documented wherever practical;
- operators can discover the information required to maintain the installed system;
- production deployments remain traceable to their release history;
- deployment is complete only after operational verification succeeds.

These principles ensure that production changes remain reproducible, understandable, recoverable, and maintainable without depending on undocumented knowledge of the original developer or deploying engineer.

# 7. Operational Policies

Operational Policies define the principles and responsibilities governing how RAH Lab validates, deploys, operates, updates, recovers, and maintains hospital software and supporting infrastructure.

These policies focus on operational discipline, consistency, traceability, protection of production state, and long-term maintainability. They apply across RAH Lab operations regardless of the application or technology involved.

Detailed software engineering, release construction, infrastructure engineering, and implementation requirements are defined by their corresponding standards and playbooks.

---

## 7.1 Engineering and Production Separation

Engineering activities shall be performed within the appropriate RAH Engineering Laboratory environment before changes are introduced into production.

The Engineering Laboratory provides the controlled environments required to:

- develop and modify software;
- prepare application artifacts;
- prepare Application Releases;
- engineer Infrastructure Releases;
- validate software and infrastructure;
- reproduce deployment procedures;
- prepare required technical and operational documentation.

The Offline Production Environment shall not be used as a software development or release-construction environment.

Production changes shall originate from approved and identifiable release artifacts rather than from uncontrolled modification of the live system.

---

## 7.2 Validation Policy

No Application Release or Infrastructure Release shall be introduced into the Offline Production Environment without completing the validation appropriate to that release.

Validation may include:

- engineering and functional validation;
- stakeholder or customer validation where required;
- offline installation or update validation;
- infrastructure validation;
- database installation or migration validation;
- production-access-path validation;
- production verification after deployment.

Validation shall reflect the actual conditions relevant to production operation.

Where an application depends on offline assets, shared services, HTTPS, trusted certificates, browser permissions, microphone access, database connectivity, or other environment-dependent functionality, those capabilities shall be validated through a representative operational path rather than assumed from development testing alone.

Production deployment is considered complete only after the required post-deployment verification succeeds.

---

## 7.3 Change Management

Every production change shall follow a controlled release-driven workflow.

The standard operational path is:

```text
Engineering Change
        │
        ▼
Validation
        │
        ▼
Versioned Release
        │
        ▼
Approved Offline Transfer
        │
        ▼
Production Installation or Update
        │
        ▼
Operational Verification
        │
        ▼
Deployment Record
```

Application software, database structures, infrastructure components, and deployment configuration shall not normally be changed directly in production outside this process.

When an urgent corrective action is unavoidable, the intervention shall be documented, its effect on the deployed state shall be understood, and the correction shall subsequently be incorporated into the appropriate controlled engineering and release process.

The objective is to prevent production systems from evolving into undocumented states that cannot be reproduced from approved releases.

---

## 7.4 Configuration and Credential Management

Application artifacts shall remain independent from deployment-specific configuration wherever practical.

Environment-specific information may include:

- server addresses;
- database connections;
- service endpoints;
- network ports;
- machine and service credentials;
- authentication settings;
- API or AI service endpoints;
- other deployment-specific settings.

Such configuration shall be maintained through approved external configuration mechanisms rather than requiring modification or rebuilding of application images.

For deployed applications, persistent runtime configuration shall remain associated with the stable live installation under:

```text
/opt/rah/apps/<app-slug>/
```

Persistent configuration shall survive versioned Application Release updates and shall not depend on manually copying configuration from one Release Package directory to another.

Machine and service credentials may be generated automatically during installation using values compatible with the application's technology stack.

Where an application provides an operator-facing administrative account, an approved predictable initial credential may be used when operationally appropriate.

Information required by the RAH Lab engineer to operate or maintain the deployment shall be made discoverable through the protected operational documentation rather than depending on memory, source-code inspection, container inspection, or previous engineering conversations.

---

## 7.5 Production Data Protection

Production hospital information is persistent operational state and shall be protected throughout the complete application lifecycle.

Accordingly:

- production data shall remain within the approved Offline Production Environment unless an explicitly authorized procedure permits otherwise;
- production databases shall not be recreated as a normal application-update mechanism;
- application container replacement or recreation shall not delete persistent production data;
- database migrations shall preserve existing operational information unless an explicitly approved data transformation requires otherwise;
- production data shall not be copied into online engineering environments except through an authorized procedure;
- access to production systems and operational data shall be restricted to authorized personnel;
- changes affecting persistent production state shall have an appropriate backup and recovery strategy.

Protection of production information takes priority over deployment convenience.

---

## 7.6 Backup and Recovery

Production changes that may affect persistent state or operational availability shall have an appropriate recovery strategy.

Depending on the operation, recovery materials may include:

- production database backups;
- persistent application configuration;
- previous Application Releases;
- required container images;
- applicable Infrastructure Releases;
- database migration recovery information;
- application-specific backup artifacts;
- documented restoration procedures.

Backups shall be created before changes that may modify or endanger existing production data.

A fresh installation of a new empty database does not require a backup of that nonexistent state; once operational information exists, subsequent changes shall follow the applicable backup requirements.

Rollback shall restore a known operational state rather than merely reverse individual commands.

Failed deployments, recovery actions, and resulting operational state shall be documented.

---

## 7.7 Operational Documentation Policy

RAH Lab operations shall leave behind sufficient documentation for another authorized engineer to understand and maintain the deployed environment without depending on undocumented personal knowledge.

The standardized operational documentation root is:

```text
/opt/rah/documentation/
```

This directory serves as the RAH operational **Obsidian Vault** and contains human-readable deployment and operational information.

Each deployed application shall maintain an identifiable documentation area containing the information required to understand its current deployment and history.

Depending on the application, operational documentation may include:

- current deployed version;
- installation and update dates;
- live deployment location;
- application access information;
- operator-facing credentials;
- database connection information and required operational credentials;
- DBeaver connection identity;
- relevant backup locations;
- applied database migrations;
- deployment verification results;
- release history;
- known operational considerations.

Installation and update procedures should generate or update this information automatically wherever practical.

Because the Vault may contain credentials, network information, deployment details, and other sensitive operational information, it shall be treated as controlled operational data and protected accordingly.

Technical architecture, source-code documentation, engineering design decisions, and release-construction documentation remain governed by their respective project documentation, standards, and playbooks and do not need to be duplicated in the operational Vault unless they are required for ongoing operation.

---

## 7.8 Operational Responsibility

The RAH Lab engineer is responsible for maintaining the operational integrity of systems deployed through RAH Lab.

Typical responsibilities include:

- receiving and identifying approved release artifacts;
- validating releases before production deployment;
- performing controlled production installations and updates;
- verifying applications after deployment;
- maintaining production data continuity;
- ensuring required backups and recovery information exist;
- confirming operational database access through approved tools such as DBeaver;
- maintaining deployment documentation and release history;
- troubleshooting operational failures without introducing uncontrolled production changes;
- performing or coordinating rollback and recovery when required;
- maintaining the stability and traceability of deployed systems.

The operator is not expected to reconstruct undocumented engineering decisions during routine maintenance. Release artifacts, engineering standards, deployment tooling, and operational documentation shall provide the information required to perform normal operational responsibilities.

---

## 7.9 Continuous Improvement

RAH Lab operational practices are expected to evolve as deployment experience exposes recurring failure modes, unnecessary manual work, or opportunities for improved reliability.

Improvements may target:

- reliability;
- maintainability;
- security;
- operational simplicity;
- repeatability;
- recoverability;
- deployment automation;
- documentation quality;
- reduction of unnecessary operator intervention.

Recurring manual tasks should be evaluated for standardization or automation when doing so makes deployment and maintenance more deterministic.

Lessons discovered while deploying or maintaining an individual application should not automatically become institution-wide policy. They should first be evaluated to determine whether they represent:

- a project-specific issue;
- an implementation pattern;
- a reusable operational requirement;
- or a general RAH engineering standard.

Approved improvements shall then be incorporated into the appropriate canonical standard, playbook, reference, or operational procedure rather than accumulated as overlapping standalone documentation.

The objective of continuous improvement is to strengthen RAH Lab while keeping its operating model understandable, reproducible, and maintainable over time.
